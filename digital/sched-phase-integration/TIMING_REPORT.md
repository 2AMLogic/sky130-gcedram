# Scheduler/sequencer timing report (PROPOSED, behavioral)

Issue #135. Generated by `run_tests.sh`; logs in
[`results/run_tests_2026-10-10.txt`](results/run_tests_2026-10-10.txt) and
[`results/run_mutation_2026-10-10.txt`](results/run_mutation_2026-10-10.txt)
(Icarus Verilog 13.0, pinned `v13_0`). Time base 1 cycle = 1 ns is an
ASSUMPTION inherited from the scheduler. All phase durations are ASSUMPTION or
SOURCED-by-deck as tagged in `../phase-control/CONTRACT.md`. Scheduler:
`T_ROW` = `T_ACC` = 34, 32 rows, ratified `INTERVAL` 5029. Nothing here is
analog restoration, CDC, synthesis or sign-off evidence, and no spec or
production module was changed.

## 1. Observed boundary (anchored 2+10+20+2, GAP=0)

The scheduler registers `op_busy`/`op_row`/kind on its decision edge. The
adapter turns the first `op_busy` cycle into `start`; the sequencer registers
it, so its `busy` rises one cycle after the scheduler's. Cycle trace of one
REFRESH (row 0) from `+TRACE` (cycle numbers from the harness; row 1 launches
immediately after):

```
cyc  scheduler busy/done   adapter start  sequencer busy/done  strobe
  5  busy=1                start=1 (row0) busy=0
  6  busy=1                               busy=1               pre_en
 38  busy=1                               busy=1               bl_drive (wwl_en fell)
 39  busy=0 done=1                        busy=1               bl_drive  <- final active strobe
 40  busy=1 (row1 launch)  start=1 (row1) busy=0 done=1         (start accepted in the done cycle)
 41  busy=1                               busy=1               pre_en (row1)
```

The scheduler declares row 0 complete (busy low, `op_done`) in cycle 39 while
the sequencer is still driving `bl_drive` and is busy; the sequencer's `done`
is cycle 40, the same cycle the next launch arrives, which is accepted
because the sequencer is idle in its `done` cycle. The margin is exactly zero.

READ and WRITE are `ok` in `anchored`, `gap1`, `full_read_pulse`, `fast`

Checks that held in every case (no `VIOLATION`): one start per launch, correct
kind and row captured for every accepted operation, no duplicated operation,
observed strobe counts and busy length equal to the sequencer parameters, no
strobe while idle, scheduler row round-robin, `start_ignored` exactly on
start-while-busy. Any launch that was not accepted is a counted `LOST`; none
was silent.

Scenario outcomes (anchored): 0 isolated READ/WRITE and 32-row sweep:
36 launches, 36 executed. 1 back-to-back mix: 143/143. 2 arbitration: 343/343,
96 REFRESH rows interleaved with traffic, zero margin each time. 3
configuration transitions (floor shorten, lengthen, forced sweep, mid-op
snapshot, infeasible refusal with `cfg_reject`, disable, re-enable, sweep
during refresh): 558/558, 287 REFRESH. 4 reset interruption: two in-flight
operations (one READ-class, one REFRESH) are abandoned, both modules idle with
no completion, the post-reset sweep restarts at row 0 (round-robin check holds).

## 3. Mutation results

All 10 mutants are killed by a scoreboard `VIOLATION` on top of the passing
baseline: dropped launch (refresh and access), wrong row (refresh and
access), duplicated start (refresh and access), and four early-completion
sequencer variants (short guard, short sense window, no guard after
write-back, short write-back pulse).

## 4. Findings and PROPOSED integration contract

Not decided here; the fixed-duration scheduler and the phase ordering are
retained, and the analog overlap question is untouched.

1. **F1 (anchored): one-cycle completion skew.** The scheduler's
   `op_busy` falls and `op_done` pulses one cycle before the sequencer's final
   `bl_drive` cycle and `done`. Equal numeric durations (34 = 34) do not mean
   the operations are aligned: the sequencer starts one cycle after the
   scheduler's launch. Zero margin to the next launch. Any consumer of the
   scheduler's `op_done` as "strobes finished" is off by one.
2. **F2 (GAP 1, full_read_pulse, mismatch): lost launches.** A REFRESH longer
   than `T_ROW` + 1 makes the next launch hit a busy sequencer; it is ignored
   and flagged but the scheduler has already recorded the row as refreshed
   (`last_ref`, pointer advance, `sweep_done`). Half the rows of a sweep are
   never actually refreshed: a retention hazard, not a cosmetic one.
3. **F3: READ/WRITE have slack, REFRESH does not.** Against `T_ACC` there are
   9 to 27 cycles of slack in every set except `mismatch` WRITE.
4. **F4: reset abandons in-flight work.** No completion, no recovery of the
   partially driven row; out of scope here.

PROPOSED contract (for a separate, approved proposal):

* C1. Scheduler-reported completion must not precede the sequencer's `done`:
  either completion-driven (scheduler waits on `phase_seq.done`, deadline
  reasoning to be redone) or fixed-budget with the explicit constraint
  `T_ROW >= sequencer REFRESH cycles + 1` and `T_ACC >= READ/WRITE cycles + 1`
  (the +1 is the adapter/registration lag measured here).
* C2. An elaboration-time or bench assertion tying scheduler constants to the
  sequencer parameters, so a phase change cannot silently create F2.
* C3. A launch while the sequencer is busy must be an error flag visible to
  the scheduler (today `start_ignored` is unobserved by it), not a silent loss.
* C4. A reset-interruption policy (re-refresh of the interrupted row after
  reset) is an open item for the analog and controller designers.

Until C1 or C2 lands, no timing claim connecting scheduler `T_ROW` to
sequencer phases may be made for any set other than `anchored`, and for
`anchored` only with the one-cycle skew and zero margin recorded above.

## 5. Follow-up (appended, issue #138; sections 1-4 above are unchanged evidence)

The fixed-budget remedy proposed in C1/C2/C3 is implemented as a separate
coupled configuration and verified in [`COUPLED_REPORT.md`](COUPLED_REPORT.md)
(`T_ROW = D_REF + 1`, `T_ACC = max(D_RD, D_WR) + 1`, configuration gate, executed
completion monitors). The F1/F2 findings above remain the characterization of
the uncoupled harness and are still reproduced by `run_tests.sh`. The coupled
work also found that the #74/#93 interval floor needs the per-operation decision
cycle for these durations (COUPLED_REPORT.md Sec. 1).
